Written below is a brief summary of the skills I picked up during my time at Kenna

Employer Description

Kenna is a "one stop shop" for businesses, meaning it provides a large suite of services for its clients, including applications development, accounts, marketing, and more. Kenna is a child company of Stagwell, in charge of calculating incentives and rewards for farmers to recieve if they meet the right criteria. The division I was in focused on creating web applications to assist with the calculation of incentive payments, and displaying information about each client.

Goals

At my third term at Kenna, I set the following goals: 1. Develop my problem solving skills to tackle each new problem uniquely in a way that best fits the situation. 2. Continue to develop my SQL skills. 3. Develop my knowledge on company procedures and standard year over year work.

Job Description

I started off this semester by developing rewards calculations for several rebates offered by BASF, a major client of Kenna. Due to the nature of dealing with real world money, it was extremely important to test thoroughly, and write accurate code with no mistakes. I was able to effectively write the procedures using old reward calculators as a template, while being careful to implement new requirements for this current years calculations. This helped me with my second goal of developing my SQL skills as the rewards calculations are done entirely in SQL. The SQL stored procedure I wrote did it's job well, while remaining accurate and human-readable. Additionally, this project was probably the most helpful for goal 3, as it taught me much of the companies year over year work, rather than working on larger, more permenant projects.


My next project was assisting with the rebuild of a major Kenna app. Many of the internal tools used at Kenna were written are largely outdated, and a recurring project during the time I've spent as a coop student has been to assist with the various rebuild projects. This semester I largely worked on a tool called Channel Connect, which allows BASF employees to track and update retailer/distributor data such as inventories, POG Plans, Purchase Plans, and much more. I rebuilt several pages for this app, including Opening/Ending Inventory (read only pages displaying many metrics on distributors) and Allocation. The Allocation page was significantly more work, as it was a multi-level grid that was read/write. It allows for the priority allocation of products to certain distributors in cases when the demand exceeds the supply of a product.


My last major project was the full rebuild of a smaller tool, Issue Resolution. I was in charge of building Issue Resolution from the ground up, using the same tech stack as many of the other tools developed and used at Kenna. This project allowed me to participate in as much of the full software life cycle as possible in a short term, from setting up the programming environment, to developing the backend, to developing the api, and finally creating the frontent. During the whole process, I was communicating with the QA and Reporting teams, gathering and implementing requirements into the app.

Conclusion

In conclusion, during my time working for Kenna, I was able to learn a lot about a more corporate structure of programming, as well as gain valuble industry skills.
